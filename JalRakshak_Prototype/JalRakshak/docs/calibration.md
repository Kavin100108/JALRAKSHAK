# JalRakshak Sensor Calibration Guide

Accurate sensor calibration is fundamental to the reliability of any early-warning monitoring system. Because low-cost analog sensor modules exhibit unit-to-unit variance and temperature drift, **raw factory values should never be used without physical calibration against verified reference solutions**.

---

## 1. pH Sensor Calibration (Two-Point Method)

### 1.1 Equipment Required
- Standard reference buffer solutions: **pH 7.00 (or 6.86)** and **pH 4.01** (available as powder sachets or premixed laboratory buffers).
- Deionized or distilled water in a wash bottle.
- Clean lint-free wipes (Kimwipes or soft tissue).
- Glass beaker or clean plastic cup.

### 1.2 Underlying Science: The Nernst Equation
The theoretical potential generated by a glass combination pH electrode follows the Nernst equation:
$$E = E_0 - \frac{2.303 \cdot R \cdot T}{F} \cdot (\text{pH} - \text{pH}_0)$$
Where:
- $\frac{2.303 \cdot R \cdot T}{F} \approx 59.16\text{ mV / pH unit}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$.
- The pH signal conditioner module amplifies and offsets this tiny millivolt potential into an analog voltage ($0.0 - 5.0\text{V}$, or $0.0 - 3.3\text{V}$ after voltage division).

We model the sensor transfer function linearly as:
$$\text{pH} = m \cdot V + c$$
Where $m$ is the slope ($\Delta\text{pH}/\Delta V$) and $c$ is the intercept.

### 1.3 Step-by-Step Procedure
1. **Rinse & Dry:** Rinse the glass bulb thoroughly with distilled water and gently blot dry with a lint-free tissue (do NOT rub the glass bulb, as friction creates static charge).
2. **Buffer 1 Measurement (Neutral: pH 7.00):**
   - Submerge the probe into the **pH 7.00** buffer.
   - Stir gently for 10 seconds and allow the reading to stabilize for 60 seconds.
   - Observe the raw voltage reading on the Serial Monitor (`rawVolts.ph`).
   - Record this value as $V_7$ (e.g., $1.520\text{ V}$).
3. **Buffer 2 Measurement (Acidic: pH 4.01):**
   - Rinse the probe thoroughly with distilled water and blot dry.
   - Submerge the probe into the **pH 4.01** buffer.
   - Allow reading to stabilize for 60 seconds.
   - Record the raw voltage on the Serial Monitor as $V_4$ (e.g., $2.045\text{ V}$).
4. **Calculate Calibration Constants:**
   $$m = \frac{\text{pH}_4 - \text{pH}_7}{V_4 - V_7} = \frac{4.01 - 7.00}{V_4 - V_7} = \frac{-2.99}{V_4 - V_7}$$
   $$c = 7.00 - (m \cdot V_7)$$
5. **Update Firmware (`config.h`):**
   ```cpp
   #define PH_CALIBRATION_SLOPE      -5.70f   // Replace with your calculated 'm'
   #define PH_CALIBRATION_INTERCEPT  15.50f   // Replace with your calculated 'c'
   ```

## 2. Turbidity Sensor Calibration

### 2.1 Principle of Operation
Optical turbidity sensors emit infrared light ($850\text{nm}$) across a liquid gap to a phototransistor. Suspended particulate matter scatters the light:
- **Clean, clear water:** Maximum light reaches the detector $\rightarrow$ highest voltage output ($\approx 4.0\text{V} - 4.5\text{V}$).
- **Turbid, muddy water:** Particulates scatter light away from detector $\rightarrow$ voltage output drops.

### 2.2 Calibration Procedure
1. **Zero Point (Pure Clear Water - 0 NTU):**
   - Fill a clean transparent vessel with freshly opened distilled or deionized water.
   - Place the optical sensor head into the distilled water. Ensure no bubbles cling to the optical lenses.
   - Record the voltage as $V_{\text{clear}}$ (e.g., $2.95\text{ V}$ after voltage divider).
2. **Span Point (Turbid Reference Standard):**
   - If a commercial Formazin turbidity standard (e.g., 100 NTU) is unavailable, a secondary field suspension can be prepared using standard calibrated kaolin or a calibrated handheld turbidity meter reading.
   - Record the voltage as $V_{\text{turbid}}$ and the corresponding NTU reading as $\text{NTU}_{\text{ref}}$.
3. **Calculate Calibration Parameters:**
   $$m_{\text{turb}} = \frac{\text{NTU}_{\text{ref}} - 0.0}{V_{\text{turbid}} - V_{\text{clear}}}$$
   $$c_{\text{turb}} = 0.0 - (m_{\text{turb}} \cdot V_{\text{clear}})$$
4. **Update Firmware (`config.h`):**
   ```cpp
   #define TURB_CALIBRATION_SLOPE    -120.0f
   #define TURB_CALIBRATION_OFFSET   350.0f
   ```

## 3. Electrical Conductivity (EC / TDS) Calibration

### 3.1 Standard Solution
Conductivity calibration requires a standard reference solution of known specific conductance:
- **Primary Standard:** **$1413\ \mu\text{S/cm}$ Potassium Chloride (KCl)** standard solution at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$.

### 3.2 Temperature Normalization
Water conductivity increases significantly with temperature (typically $+1.9\%$ to $+2.1\%$ per $^\circ\text{C}$) due to increased ionic mobility. JalRakshak dynamically normalizes all readings to the international reference temperature of **$25.0^\circ\text{C}$**:
$$\text{EC}_{25} = \frac{\text{EC}_{\text{measured}}}{1.0 + \alpha \cdot (T - 25.0)}$$
Where:
- $\alpha = 0.020$ ($2.0\%/^\circ\text{C}$ standard temperature coefficient).
- $T$ is the instantaneous water temperature measured by the DS18B20 probe.

### 3.3 Calibration Steps
1. Submerge both the EC probe and the DS18B20 temperature probe into the $1413\ \mu\text{S/cm}$ calibration solution.
2. Wait 2 minutes for temperature equilibrium.
3. Observe `rawVolts.ec` and the measured temperature on the Serial Monitor.
4. Calculate the scale factor:
   $$\text{Scale Factor} = \frac{1413\ \mu\text{S/cm} \cdot [1.0 + 0.020 \cdot (T - 25.0)]}{V_{\text{measured}}}$$
5. Update `config.h`:
   ```cpp
   #define EC_CALIBRATION_FACTOR     1000.0f // Replace with calculated factor
   #define EC_TEMP_COEFFICIENT       0.020f
   ```

## 4. Temperature Sensor Validation (DS18B20)

The Dallas DS18B20 is a pre-calibrated factory digital sensor with a rated accuracy of $\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ from $-10^\circ\text{C}$ to $+85^\circ\text{C}$. Calibration involves validating accuracy at zero and ambient:

1. **Ice Bath Check ($0.0^\circ\text{C}$):**
   - Fill a thermal flask with crushed ice cubes made from pure water and add just enough cold water to fill the gaps between ice chips.
   - Stir and immerse the stainless-steel probe for 3 minutes.
   - The reading should stabilize between $-0.3^\circ\text{C}$ and $+0.3^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Field Offset Trim:**
   - If the reading deviates by a constant offset $\Delta T$, configure `TEMP_OFFSET_C` in `config.h`:
   ```cpp
   #define TEMP_OFFSET_C             -0.2f  // Trims constant residual error
   ```

## 5. Routine Maintenance & Recalibration Schedule

| Sensor | Recommended Recalibration Frequency | Cleaning Procedure | Storage Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **pH Electrode** | Every **14 to 30 days** | Soak in $0.1\text{M HCl}$ for 5 minutes if fouled; rinse with distilled water. Never scrape glass! | Must be kept moist in **3M KCl** storage solution. Never store in distilled water! |
| **Turbidity Optical** | Every **30 to 60 days** | Gently wipe optical lenses with cotton swab dipped in isopropyl alcohol. | Clean and dry when removed from liquid. |
| **EC Probe** | Every **60 to 90 days** | Clean metal electrode pins with 70% alcohol; use soft brush to remove biofilm. | Dry storage. |
| **DS18B20** | Semi-annually | Wipe stainless steel sheath with clean rag. | Ambient dry. |
